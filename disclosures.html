ApolloQLabs – Disclosures and Disclaimers

Standards this wording is built on

  • FTC Guides Concerning the Use of Endorsements and Testimonials in Advertising, 16 CFR Part 255 (as revised 2023), especially §255.5 (disclosure of material connections) and the definition of "clearly and conspicuously" in §255.0.
  • FINRA Rule 2210 (communications must be fair and balanced, give a sound basis for evaluating the facts, not omit material facts, not be promissory, exaggerated or misleading, and generally not predict or project performance) and FINRA Rule 2220 (options communications, including the reference to the options disclosure document).
  • The Options Clearing Corporation's Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options (the "ODD").

How disclosures appear in ApolloQLabs

  1. A disclosure must be clear and conspicuous: unavoidable, easy to read, in plain language, in a contrasting color, and placed next to the ad or link it explains. It must not be hidden behind a "more" link, in a footer only, or in a hover/tooltip.
  2. On mobile, the label must be visible without scrolling or tapping, on the same screen as the sponsored content.
  3. The label cannot be removed by configuration (the app already enforces this for the "Sponsored" chip).
  4. Sponsored content must look different from ApolloQLabs' own content and from model output, so it cannot be mistaken for an ApolloQLabs analysis, signal or recommendation.
  5. Use only creatives supplied or approved by the advertiser's compliance team, unedited, and keep a dated record of each creative, its approval and where it ran (at least 3 years).
  6. Reject creatives that promise or imply returns, use "guaranteed," "risk-free," "can't lose," "easy money," or income claims, or omit material risks.
  7. Never place a sponsored item inside, or styled like, an options chain value, model output, Learn lesson text, or a proprietary ApolloQLabs estimate.

4. Risk of options trading

Short version (next to options chains, calculators and strategy tools)

Options involve significant risk and are not suitable for all investors. You can lose your entire investment quickly, and some strategies (such as selling uncovered options) can lose more than you invest. Model values are not quotes. Read Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options before trading options.

Long version (About / Legal page and Learn tab)

Options risk disclosure. Options trading involves significant risk and is not suitable for all investors. Before buying or selling any option, you should read Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options, published by The Options Clearing Corporation and available at theocc.com.

- You can lose your entire investment. An option is a wasting asset; it can expire worthless, and a buyer can lose the full premium paid, often within a short time.

- Some strategies have large or unlimited risk. Selling (writing) uncovered calls has theoretically unlimited risk; selling uncovered puts can lose up to the full strike value less the premium received. Losses can exceed the amount you deposit.

- Leverage cuts both ways. Small moves in the underlying security can cause large percentage changes in an option's value.

- Assignment and exercise. Option sellers may be assigned at any time before expiration (for American-style options), which can create unexpected positions, margin calls or tax consequences.

- Multi-leg strategies (spreads, straddles, condors and others) can involve higher total commissions and fees, legs that cannot be filled or closed at expected prices, and risks that differ from those of each leg alone.

- Liquidity, volatility and pricing. Bid/ask spreads can be wide, prices can gap, trading can be halted, and implied volatility can change sharply. Model prices, Greeks and payoff diagrams in ApolloQLabs are theoretical, rest on assumptions that may not hold, and ignore commissions, fees, taxes, dividends and early exercise unless stated.

- Margin. Many options strategies require a margin account. Margin increases risk, and a broker can sell your positions without notice if equity falls below requirements.

- Taxes. Options transactions can have complex tax consequences. Consult a tax professional.

Hypothetical or simulated results, including model chains, backtests, seasonality statistics and proprietary ApolloQLabs estimates, have inherent limitations: they do not reflect actual trading, and they may not account for market factors such as liquidity and costs. No representation is made that any account will or is likely to achieve results similar to those shown.
